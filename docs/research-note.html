Research note: How home loan approval works in an Indian bank

Deliverable D5 · Product in scope: Home loan (purchase of ready residential property) · POV demo: Sanctum · Synthetic data only · ~2 pages

1. Why this process matters

Home loans are long-tenor, collateral-backed products. Turnaround time (TAT), consistency across branches, and regulatory compliance (KYC, LTV, fair practices) all affect customer experience and credit risk. Much of the work is still document-heavy and manual: reading PDFs, reconciling income, calling bureaus, and checking property charges before a credit officer sanctions.

2. End-to-end stages (application → disbursement)

StageWho owns itWhat happens
1. Origination / applicationCustomer, Relationship Manager (RM), digital channelProduct selected; personal, income and property details captured; indicative eligibility / EMI discussed.
2. Document collection & KYCRM / operationsPAN, Aadhaar, photos, address proof; salary slips / Form 16 / ITRs; bank statements; property documents (sale deed, title chain, tax receipts, approved plan).
3. Credit appraisalCredit analyst / underwriterIncome assessment, FOIR / repayment capacity, bureau score & tradelines, existing obligations, employment / business stability.
4. Property due diligenceLegal + technical valuation panelsTitle search / legal opinion; valuation report; CERSAI search for prior charges; project / builder checks where relevant.
5. SanctionCredit officer / sanctioning authority (delegated limits)Approve, approve with conditions, or decline. Sanction letter with rate, tenure, conditions, validity.
6. Agreement, mortgage, CERSAILegal / opsLoan agreement; creation of mortgage / charge; registration of charge with CERSAI as applicable.
7. DisbursementOps / treasuryOften in tranches (under-construction) or lump sum (ready property); linked to compliance with sanction conditions.

3. Roles typically involved

  • Applicant / co-applicant — provides data and documents; may be salaried or self-employed.
  • RM / sales — originates the case, explains product, pushes for completeness.
  • Credit / underwriting — risk decision and recommendation up the sanction ladder.
  • Legal counsel / empaneled lawyers — title and documentation.
  • Valuer — market / forced-sale value of collateral.
  • Ops / disbursement team — post-sanction checklist and release of funds.

4. Key checks and policy anchors

  • KYC / AML — per RBI Know Your Customer directions; identity and address verification before account / loan relationships deepen.
  • LTV (loan-to-value) — RBI has prescribed LTV caps for housing loans by ticket size bands (commonly cited: up to 90% for smaller tickets, stepping down for larger loans). Banks embed these in credit policy.
  • FOIR / income norms — bank-specific ceilings on fixed obligations vs income; treatment differs for salaried vs self-employed.
  • Bureau — CIBIL / other CIC score, delinquencies, enquiries, unsecured exposure.
  • CERSAI — search for existing security interest on the property before creating a fresh charge.
  • Fair Practices Code — transparent terms, timely communication of decision and reasons for decline where required.

5. Documents (typical home loan file)

Identity & KYC: PAN, Aadhaar / OVD, photographs, address proof.
Income (salaried): 3–6 months salary slips, Form 16, bank statements showing salary credits.
Income (self-employed): 2–3 years ITR with computation, GST returns, business bank statements, CA-certified financials where asked.
Property: title documents, chain of ownership, tax paid receipts, approved plan / occupancy where applicable, society NOC if needed.

6. Typical turnaround and pain points

Industry conversations and secondary sources commonly put clean salaried home-loan files in a range of roughly a few days to 2–3 weeks from complete documentation to sanction, and longer when legal/title issues or incomplete papers appear. Self-employed and complex collateral stretch further.

Pain points observed / reported:

  • Incomplete or inconsistent documents (income declared ≠ bank credits).
  • Manual re-keying between LOS, CBS, and email attachments.
  • Policy interpretation varies by branch / officer → inconsistent outcomes.
  • Waiting on external parties (lawyer, valuer, bureau) without a single status view.
  • Re-work when RBI circulars or internal policy change mid-pipeline.
Implication for agentic AI: highest value is in document completeness checks, multi-source assembly (CBS + bureau + CERSAI + policy), explained recommendations, and escalation to humans — not in removing the credit officer from sanction.
